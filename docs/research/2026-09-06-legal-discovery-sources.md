# Evidence for structured legal ingestion and discovery

Research date: 2026-09-06. This note supports an architectural comparison, not an implementation decision or a measurement of GetQHSE. The studies below concern primarily US legal research; their percentages must not be presented as predictions for Moroccan QHSE applicability.

## Findings from primary sources

1. **Legal structure materially affects interpretation.** STARA annotates statutory hierarchy, defined terms, and cross-references before classifying provisions. The paper explains that subsections may be sentence fragments whose meaning depends on parent provisions, while relevant definitions may be elsewhere. Its pipeline combines broad candidate filtering with LLM analysis of contextualized provisions. This supports the proposed parent/child structure, but also suggests that a tree alone is insufficient: definitions and cross-references matter. [Surani et al., STARA, ICAIL 2025](https://dho.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/STARA.pdf), especially sections 2.2 and 3.

2. **Retrieval and legal reasoning are separate failure points.** A 2026 LaborBench evaluation reports STARA at 83% Boolean accuracy, versus 70% for a prior standard RAG baseline; after examining omissions in the human reference data, the authors report 92% for STARA. They identify both missed provisions and reasoning mistakes involving related concepts and statutory exceptions. These are benchmark-specific results, including a corrected reference set, not a universal accuracy comparison. [Afane et al., Benchmarking Legal RAG, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03300).

3. **Adding retrieval does not guarantee correctness.** A study of legal research products found hallucination rates of 17–33% in the tested systems, despite improvements over a general-purpose GPT-4 comparator. The evaluation concerns the products and versions tested at that time; it does not establish present-day ChatGPT or Claude reliability. A plausible legal citation and a retrieved document are insufficient evidence that the document supports the proposition. [Magesh et al., Hallucination-Free?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20362).

4. **Exact and semantic retrieval serve different needs.** Microsoft's search documentation describes hybrid search as parallel full-text and vector retrieval, merged with reciprocal rank fusion. Vector matching supports conceptual similarity; keyword search supports precise identifiers and specialized terms. Inference for this project: combine semantic discovery with canonical law-number lookup, rather than requiring the model to remember every correct identifier before the corpus can be searched. [Microsoft, Hybrid search overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/search/hybrid-search-overview).

5. **LLM extraction can retain evidence locations.** Google's LangExtract demonstrates structured extraction mapped to source character spans and explicitly documents ungrounded extractions with missing character intervals. Its examples favor verbatim extraction text. Inference for this project: preserve the original legal text and source location, and treat generated tags and interpretation as additional annotations. A located span proves traceability, not that a generated category or applicability judgment is correct. [Google, LangExtract README](https://github.com/google/langextract/blob/main/README.md).

## Architectural implications (inferences, not benchmark results)

- Model knowledge is useful as one source of search candidates. An unverified remembered law should remain a lead to investigate until its identity, source, version and applicable provisions have been checked. Do not make successful model recall a prerequisite for corpus retrieval.
- A missing corpus match is a coverage gap, not evidence that a law does not exist or does not apply. External discovery can help close that gap, but a remembered citation alone cannot establish an obligation.
- Preserve both document hierarchy and relationships across provisions. Expand relevant parents, scoped definitions, exceptions and explicit references; indiscriminately retrieving every sibling can add noise and processing cost.
- LLM-assisted ingestion moves repeatable enrichment work to upload time. Online model-led discovery adds inference and verification work to each assessment. Actual accuracy, latency and cost require measurements on this corpus and profiles; none of these sources supplies valid forecasts for this platform.
- Evaluate extraction completeness, retrieval recall, correct law/version identification, applicability reasoning and citation support separately. Compare the same profiles against an adjudicated reference set, and examine apparent model errors for reference-set omissions.
